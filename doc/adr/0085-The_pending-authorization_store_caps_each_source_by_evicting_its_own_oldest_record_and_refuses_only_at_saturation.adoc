= ADR-0085: The pending-authorization store caps each source by evicting its own oldest record and refuses only at saturation

// adr-metadata
// summary: InMemoryPendingAuthorizationStore admits a pending login in a fixed order: purge every expired record in expiry order, then let a quota key that holds MAX_PENDING_PER_SOURCE (256) live records evict only its own oldest one, then refuse a net-new record with PENDING_AUTHORIZATION_SATURATED (503, Retry-After 30) once the store holds its capacity, otherwise insert. A login-initiation rate limiter and moving the pushed authorization request after the store stay open decisions.

== Status

Accepted

== Decision

=== The question

Every login, step-up and session widening stores one pending-authorization record — `state`, nonce,
PKCE verifier and return URL — until the callback consumes it (ADR-0018). The record is created by a
browser that has not authenticated yet, so the store is an unauthenticated write path. An unbounded store
is a memory amplifier, and a bounded store that makes room by evicting the oldest record lets one
requester flush every other user's login in flight: the victim's callback then finds no record and the
login fails.

The store therefore needs a bound that holds memory flat under a flood *and* a rule for which record
gives way that never lets one source touch another source's live login.

=== The decision

*`InMemoryPendingAuthorizationStore.store` admits a record in a fixed order, and only the flooding
source pays for its own flood.*

. *Expired first, in expiry order.* Every record expired at the given instant is purged before any live
  record is considered. The purge reads a sorted expiry index, not insertion order: a caller stamps
  `createdAt` before its pushed authorization request and stores the record after it, so a record stored
  later can expire earlier.
. *Per-source cap with own-oldest eviction.* A record is counted under its quota key — the requester
  source `PendingQuotaKeys` derives from the connection and `forwarded.trusted_proxies`. When that key
  already holds `MAX_PENDING_PER_SOURCE` (256) live records, the key's *own* oldest record is evicted and
  the new one inserted: no net growth, and no other source's record is touched. The new start is admitted.
. *Refuse only at saturation.* Otherwise, when the store holds its capacity of live records (10,000 in
  the producer), the record is refused with `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION_SATURATED`, which the edge answers
  `503` with `Retry-After: 30`. Nothing stored is touched: a full store never evicts a live record of
  another source to make room.
. Otherwise the record is inserted.

The constant is documented, not configured, like the other hardening bounds (ADR-0060, ADR-0064).

=== The two signals and their episodes

Both outcomes that a flood produces are observable, and neither lets a caller set the volume of `WARN`
lines it causes (ADR-0051). Neither record carries request data — no source address, quota key, record
id, `state` or nonce.

* *Saturation.* A refusal is counted on `sheriff_errors_total` under `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION_SATURATED`
  and logged `ApiSheriff-145` once per saturation episode, every further refusal at `DEBUG`. The latch is
  set by the first refusal and released with hysteresis: only once the store has drained below half its
  capacity. Freeing one place and filling it again keeps the episode open.
* *Own-oldest eviction.* Every own-oldest eviction hands `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION_OWN_OLDEST_EVICTED` to
  the store's event counter, which `BffRuntimeProducer` binds to `SheriffMetrics.recordSessionEvent`, so
  the eviction rate is `sheriff_session_events_total{event="PENDING_AUTHORIZATION_OWN_OLDEST_EVICTED"}`.
  The event is informational: the request is admitted, so it has no category and no HTTP mapping, is not
  a reconnaissance signal and is not in the uniform-`404` closed set. The eviction is logged
  `ApiSheriff-154` once per eviction episode and every further eviction of the episode at `DEBUG`.
  *The eviction episode is store-wide and ends only once no quota key holds half its per-source share
  (128 live records) or more.* An evicting key is at its full share, so a source cannot end the episode
  by letting one record expire, and a source that keeps flooding keeps it open. The store keeps the count
  of keys at or above half their share on every insert and removal, so the release check costs nothing
  per call.

== Alternatives rejected

=== Evict the globally oldest record when the store is full

The store would keep one bound and make room by evicting the oldest record, whoever stored it.

Rejected because it is exactly the displacement the decision exists to prevent: a single source filling
the store would flush every other user's login in flight, and each victim's callback would fail.

=== Refuse a source at its share instead of evicting its oldest record

A source at its per-source share would be refused rather than have its own oldest record evicted.

Rejected because the source's newest start is the one the user is waiting on. A user who opened many
login tabs, or a client that retries, would be locked out of a fresh login by its own stale records,
which expire only after their fixed lifetime. Evicting the source's own oldest record costs that source
alone and keeps its newest login working.

=== Reuse the saturation release rule for the eviction latch

The eviction latch would be released, like the saturation latch, once the store has drained below half
its capacity.

Rejected because the two conditions live on different scales. A source at its share of 256 leaves a
store of 10,000 far below half full, so the rule would release the latch before every eviction and log a
`WARN` for each one — a single source would set the `WARN` volume again.

=== Latch the eviction record per source

Each quota key would carry its own eviction latch.

Rejected because the set of sources is attacker-chosen: a latch per source is either unbounded state or
needs its own bound and overflow rule, and a flood from many sources would still produce one `WARN` per
source. The counter already gives the per-flood rate, and the store-wide episode gives the one line an
operator needs.

== Open decisions

Two gaps named when the fairness mechanism was reviewed stay open, by decision: this ADR does not close
them, and the threat model carries both as accepted residuals of link:../security-threat-model.adoc#bff-20[BFF-20] until a later
decision does. Each is stated with the question a closing decision has to answer.

* *No login-initiation rate limiter.* The per-source cap bounds how much of the store one source can
  hold, not how often it can start a login. A source can churn its own share indefinitely: every start
  past its share evicts one of its own records and costs one pushed authorization request at the identity
  provider. Per-route `rate_limit` is not implemented (`ApiSheriff-140`), and `enumeration_detection` does
  not count login starts. Whether the login-initiation path gets its own rate limit, and keyed how, is
  open.
* *The pushed authorization request runs before the store.* `LoginFlow`, `SessionWidening` and the
  step-up re-drive push the authorization request (ADR-0058) and only then store the pending record. A
  start the store refuses at saturation has therefore already cost the identity provider one push, and so
  has every own-oldest eviction. Checking capacity before the push would need a reservation the store does
  not offer today, because a pre-check outside the store's monitor reopens the window the monitor closes.
  Whether the store gains a reserve-then-commit contract so a refused start never reaches the identity
  provider is open.

== Consequences

* *One source's login flood cannot evict another user's live login.* Cross-source displacement does not exist
  on any path: the purge removes only expired records, the per-source cap evicts only the flooding key's
  own records, and saturation refuses instead of evicting. A source at its cap still occupies its share of
  the store's capacity, so when other sources already hold the rest, its records can bring the store to
  capacity, and new logins from any other source are then refused with `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION_SATURATED`
  while the store stays full.
* *A flooding source loses its own oldest logins in flight.* Its evicted logins' callbacks find no
  record and are refused `403`. A legitimate user who shares a source address with many others behind a proxy the gateway does
  not trust shares that source's share; `forwarded.trusted_proxies` is what gives such users their own
  quota keys.
* *Two bounded `WARN` lines per flood, at most.* `ApiSheriff-145` for a saturation episode and
  `ApiSheriff-154` for an eviction episode; the rates are on the two counters.
* *The store calls its event counter under its monitor.* The counter must not block and must not call
  back into the store. `SheriffMetrics.recordSessionEvent` only increments a cached meter.
* *`PendingAuthorizationStoreTest` pins the mechanism.* The fairness tests cover the expiry-order purge,
  the per-source eviction and the saturation refusal with its latch; the own-oldest eviction test drives
  more than `MAX_PENDING_PER_SOURCE` records under one quota key and asserts one counter increment per
  eviction, exactly one `ApiSheriff-154` per episode and `DEBUG` after.
