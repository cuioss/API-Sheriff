== Status

Proposed

== Context

The gateway re-asserts HTTP framing integrity at the edge, ahead of route selection, because framing
is what decides where one request ends and the next begins. A gateway that disagrees with its
upstream about that boundary is the precondition for request smuggling, so the framing gate rejects
several independently sufficient shapes: `Content-Length` together with `Transfer-Encoding`, a body
on a method defined as bodyless, and a `Connection` token naming a framing or trust header.

The rejection of a body on a bodyless method is the one of these that meets legitimate traffic.
`GET` is defined as bodyless, but a body on `GET` is not universally meaningless: some upstream APIs
deliberately accept one, the search API that takes a structured query document being the widely
deployed case. A deployment fronting such an upstream finds that a request its backend understands
perfectly is rejected at the gateway, and the only workarounds available to it are worse than the
problem — rewriting the client to a different method, or disabling the framing defences wholesale.

The structural choice point is what the escape hatch for that traffic looks like, and the framing
gate makes the choice unusually consequential in two ways.

First, the rejection is *not one condition*. A body on a bodyless method is rejected on three
independently sufficient signals: an observed body, a positive declared `Content-Length`, and the
presence of `Transfer-Encoding`. Written as a single disjunction they are indistinguishable, and the
obvious shape for an opt-in is to gate the whole disjunction. But the three signals do not carry the
same risk. Chunked framing on a method defined as bodyless is precisely the shape the desync
defences exist to constrain — it is the primitive that makes a front-end and a back-end disagree
about message boundaries. A declared, positive `Content-Length` is not: it is an unambiguous,
self-describing framing that both ends parse identically. An opt-in that gates the disjunction
therefore buys a use case that needs one signal by admitting a different signal that the use case
never sends.

Second, the gate runs *before route selection*. That position is deliberate — framing must be settled
before the request is parsed far enough to choose a route — and it means there is no route resolved
at the moment the decision is taken. Any knob at this stage is gateway-wide whether or not its
configuration surface admits it. A per-route spelling here would be a surface that cannot be
honoured, which is worse than a gateway-wide surface that says so.

The question this record settles is how narrow a security relaxation must be cut when the rejection
it relaxes is a disjunction over signals of unequal risk, and where such a relaxation may be scoped.

== Decision

*The opt-in admits exactly one thing: a positive declared `Content-Length` on `GET`. Every other leg
of the framing gate stays unconditional, and the opt-in is gateway-wide because the gate that
implements it runs before any route exists to scope it to.*

=== The disjunction is split, and only one leg consults the opt-in

The three rejection signals are evaluated as ordered, separate checks rather than one disjunction.
`Transfer-Encoding` is evaluated first and unconditionally: no configuration relaxes chunked framing
on a bodyless method. Only the declared-`Content-Length` leg consults the opt-in, and only for `GET`.
`HEAD` is unaffected on every leg. The residual leg — a body signalled without a positive declared
`Content-Length` — falls through to the rejection, so the opt-in admits nothing that is not
`Content-Length`-framed.

The CL+TE coexistence rule and the framing-header-strip rule are separate checks invoked
independently of this one, so scoping the opt-in to the bodyless-method rejection cannot weaken
either.

The split is what makes the relaxation's bound structural. As one disjunction, "only the
`Content-Length` case is relaxed" would be a claim about the caller's inputs; as separate legs, it is
a property of the gate.

=== The gate re-asserts its own bound rather than inheriting it

The residual leg matters even where the calling stage's construction happens to make it unreachable.
A gate whose stated bound holds only because of what its caller passes has outsourced its bound, and
the guarantee then depends on a relationship neither component describes. The gate therefore
re-checks the body-present signal itself, so its stated bound is true of the gate under any input.

=== The default is off, and the surface is gateway-wide

An omitted key resolves to rejection — the behaviour of a gateway that never declared it — so the
secure posture requires no configuration and cannot be reached by accident. The key sits in the
gateway-wide defaults block and is documented as gateway-wide, because the stage it governs precedes
route selection. This is the same shape as the other pre-route relaxation the gateway carries: a
knob at a pre-route stage is honest about its blast radius or it lies about it.

== Consequences

=== Positive

* The desync primitive the framing defences exist to constrain remains unreachable by configuration:
  there is no gateway setting under which chunked framing on a bodyless method is admitted.
* The motivating traffic is served exactly, at zero cost to the constraint — a client sending a
  structured query document on `GET` sends it `Content-Length`-framed.
* The relaxation's stated blast radius is its enforced blast radius, because each admitted and each
  refused shape is a separate branch rather than a claim about a disjunction.
* A deployment that does not need the capability is unaffected without acting, and a deployment that
  enables it is told in the configuration surface that it did so gateway-wide.

=== Negative

* The knob is coarser than an operator would prefer: a deployment needing body-on-`GET` for one
  upstream enables it for every route the gateway serves. That is a genuine widening, and it is
  irreducible at this stage.
* A single conceptual rule is now several branches, which is more code and more tests than the
  disjunction it replaces, for a rejection surface where uniformity was previously easy to verify at
  a glance.
* An operator reading "allow GET with body" reasonably expects all bodies on `GET` to be admitted. The
  key's actual boundary must be carried in its name and in every document describing it, or the
  surface misleads.

=== Risks

* *Re-collapsing the legs.* The split reads as redundancy — three branches producing the same
  rejection, one of them apparently unreachable. A later simplification that merges them back into a
  disjunction gated by the opt-in silently restores the chunked admission, and it does so as a
  tidy-up with no behavioural intent.
* *Scope creep by symmetry.* `HEAD` is excluded deliberately and will read as an oversight, because
  it is the other bodyless method and the change to include it is one token. There is no use case
  requiring it, and admitting it widens the surface for nothing.
* *Drift between the boundary and its description.* The boundary lives in one gate; its description
  lives in the schema, the operator guide and the configuration reference. A narrowing applied to the
  gate alone leaves documents promising a wider relaxation than the gateway performs — and the wider
  promise is the one an operator plans against.

== Alternatives Considered

*Gate the whole disjunction with one opt-in.* The straightforward reading of "allow a body on `GET`",
needing no split and no per-leg reasoning. Rejected because it pays for the use case in a currency
the use case never asked for: admitting `Transfer-Encoding` on a bodyless method is admitting the
desync primitive itself, while the traffic being served sends a declared `Content-Length`. The
relaxation would be strictly larger than the problem with no corresponding benefit, which is the
defining shape of an over-broad security exception.

*Make the opt-in per-route.* The natural scoping: only the route fronting the upstream that needs it
would relax, and every other route keeps the strict posture. Rejected because it cannot be honoured
where the check lives. The framing gate runs before route selection, so at the moment the decision is
taken there is no route to consult. Implementing it would mean either deferring framing validation
until after routing — routing on input whose message boundaries are not yet settled, a strictly worse
exposure — or offering a per-route key that silently behaves gateway-wide, which is a surface that
lies about its blast radius.

*Leave the rejection unconditional and require clients to use a different method.* The strictest
option, keeping the gate free of configuration entirely. Rejected because it makes the gateway
unusable in front of a class of upstream it otherwise serves correctly, and the workaround it forces
is not neutral: rewriting a query-bearing `GET` as `POST` discards idempotency and cacheability, and
the deployment that cannot rewrite its clients is pushed toward disabling protections far broader
than this one.

*Detect the shape and admit it implicitly, with no configuration.* If a `Content-Length`-framed body
on `GET` is acceptable when an operator asks for it, the gate could simply accept it. Rejected
because it changes the default posture for every deployment to serve the needs of a few, and it
removes the operator's declaration of intent — which is the artefact that makes the widened surface
reviewable. A security relaxation that nobody had to ask for is one nobody knows they have.

The rejected options fall into two kinds. One kind widens the relaxation beyond the need — across
framing shapes, or across every deployment by default. The other kind refuses the relaxation outright
and pushes the deployment toward a broader workaround it will find on its own. The chosen position
cuts the exception to the exact shape of the traffic, declares the scope the stage actually enforces,
and leaves the default where an unconfigured gateway had it.

== Generalisation

When a rejection is a disjunction over several signals, an exception granted against it is granted
against *all* of them unless the disjunction is split. The diagnostic question before relaxing such a
rejection is: *which of these signals does the motivating case actually produce, and what does
admitting each of the others cost?* Signals that share a rejection rarely share a risk profile, and
the ones the use case never sends are exactly the ones an over-broad exception admits for free. The
second question is where the check runs relative to the context the exception wants to be scoped to:
a knob cannot be scoped to information the stage does not yet have, and a surface implying otherwise
is worse than one that states its true reach.
