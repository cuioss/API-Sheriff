== Status

Accepted

== Decision

=== The question

The gateway holds process-wide budgets. Examples are the general admission budget
(`edge_hardening.admission_cap`) and the relay sub-budget (`websocket_relay_cap`) of
link:0020-WebSocket_relays_hold_their_admission_permit_for_the_connection_lifetime_and_are_bounded_by_a_derived_sub-budget.adoc[ADR-0020].
A defect in how such a budget is returned does not fail any single request. It fails the gateway
after enough requests have run.

Two test lanes could own the detection of that defect class:

* *The gating integration suite.* The Failsafe suite under `-Pintegration-tests` runs on every pull
  request. A red run blocks the merge.
* *The benchmark lane.* The k6 suite under `-Pbenchmark` runs from
  `.github/workflows/benchmark.yml`. The workflow fires on a merged pull request, on a tag and on a
  manual dispatch. It cannot block the merge that caused a failure.

This record decides which lane owns the detection, and with which mechanism.

=== What the gating suite already held

The question was asked as if no lane detected the defect class. The gating suite already did, for
one instance of it.

* `WebSocketProxyIT#sequentialRelaysReturnTheirAdmissionPermitsAtTeardown` drives the dedicated
  instance `api-sheriff-ws-admission`. Its overlaid gateway configuration sets the general admission
  budget to 8 and the relay sub-budget to 2. The test opens and closes ten relays in sequence. It then
  requires `GET /proxy/get` to answer `200`. A relay that keeps either permit makes an upgrade or the
  final request fail.
* `WsAdmissionActivationWiringTest` runs under surefire. It asserts that both overlay caps stay
  below the probe's operation count. A drift of the overlay therefore fails a fast test, not only the
  slow one.

The remaining gap was narrower than the question assumed. Nobody had seen that probe fail. A probe
that has never failed may be unable to fail. This record closes the gap with a committed negative
control. The control holds the budget and observes the probe fail (see below).

=== The decision

*Resource-lifecycle and cumulative-exhaustion defects are detected in the gating Failsafe suite, by
shrunken-budget exhaustion tests. The benchmark lane stays a non-gating measurement lane for
throughput and latency.*

A shrunken-budget exhaustion test has four parts:

. *A dedicated low-budget instance.* An overlaid gateway configuration lowers the budget under test
  to single digits. Only that budget differs from the primary instance. A production-sized budget
  would need thousands of operations to run dry.
. *A probe.* The probe performs more operations than the budget allows. It then makes a claim about
  the *gateway*, not about one response. A leaked unit of budget therefore turns into a refusal the
  probe observes.
. *A wiring guard.* A surefire test reads the overlay and binds its values to the probe's own
  constants. The guard fails when the overlay stops being exhaustible by the probe.
. *A negative control and its recovery.* The control holds the budget from a test fixture and runs
  the probe. The probe must fail. The control then releases what it held and runs the probe again.
  The probe must pass. The control proves that the probe can observe an exhausted budget. The
  recovery proves that the held units caused the failure.

`WebSocketProxyIT` carries the negative control on both budgets of `api-sheriff-ws-admission`:

* *Relay sub-budget.* The control holds two established relays. To the admission pool, a held relay
  is the same as a relay that never returned its permit.
* *General admission budget.* The control holds eight in-flight requests, and no relay. Each request
  declares a body length and withholds the bytes until the control releases it.

`WsAdmissionActivationWiringTest` asserts that the overlay caps equal the held counts the controls
use. It reads those counts from `WebSocketProxyIT` rather than from a copy.

The controls prove that the probe observes an exhausted pool on either budget. They do not replay a
defect in the relay's release path. The unit tests of the relay stage own that path.

=== The three shipped instances

The decision was tested against the three resource-related failures the gateway has shipped. Each is
described by its content.

[cols="2,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Instance | Gating suite | Benchmark lane

| A WebSocket relay kept its general admission permit after a completed upgrade. A sustained run
  exhausted the pool, and the gateway answered `503` to all traffic.
| Detected by the shrunken-budget probe above. Its negative control on the general budget reproduces
  the observable effect against the fixed gateway.
| Observed the failure under load. The response was a skip property on the `websocketEcho` goal.
  The goal ran skipped while the defect stood.

| The framework's body-size limit stayed below a route's declared body cap. The framework rejected a
  large upload before the gateway pipeline ran.
| Detected per request by `LargeBodyIT`. `BodyLimitActivationWiringTest` pins the framework floor and
  the declared cap. This is a layer-boundary defect, not a cumulative one.
| Observed the failure. The response was a skip property on the `uploadLarge` goal.

| The mTLS rejection legs failed in the `jfr` lane.
| Neither detected nor prevented. The `jfr` profile's exclusion comment in `integration-tests/pom.xml`
  records the cause: that lane pointed `MtlsHandshakeIT` at the primary instance, which requires no
  client certificate, and the client offered no identity.
| Neither detected nor prevented.
|===

The table yields two findings:

* The benchmark lane observed two of the three instances. In both, its response was to skip the goal.
  A lane that cannot block the merge has no other move when a goal fails.
* The third instance is lane-conditional. It is a property of the lane that runs the test, not of the
  gateway. Neither choice of home detects it. The lane that runs a test owns it, and a lane excludes a
  test it cannot serve by name.

=== The precedent for capacity bounds

`BffPendingLoginBoundIT` already moved one capacity bound out of `-Pbenchmark` and into the gating
suite. It proves the bound of the pending-login store against the primary instance. The reasoning
was that a capacity bound is a security property, and a security property must gate the merge.

This record adopts that reasoning for every resource bound. It does not decide it again per bound.
It adds one part the precedent lacks. A bound whose production value is too large to exhaust in a
test gets a shrunken-budget instance, and the negative control proves that the probe can fail.

== Alternatives rejected

=== The benchmark lane as the detection home

Make the benchmark lane responsible for exhaustion defects, and let its failures gate the merge.

Rejected:

* *It runs after the merge.* The workflow fires on a merged pull request, on a tag and on a manual
  dispatch. A failure arrives after the defect is on `main`.
* *Making it gate is a structural change to the release lane.* The job has a budget of 75 minutes. It
  starts seven native gateway instances beside the identity provider and the upstreams. It mints a
  GitHub App token and deploys results to `cuioss.github.io` in the same job. A pull request from a
  fork receives none of the secrets that job needs. Gating every pull request on it would move a
  release-lane job into the merge path.
* *Its demonstrated reaction to an exhaustion finding was a skip.* The lane saw the relay-permit leak
  and answered with a skip property. A non-gating lane has no stronger reaction available.

=== A time-based soak test in the gating suite

Run sustained load against the production-sized budget for a fixed time, and fail when the gateway
degrades.

Rejected:

* *It lengthens the integration lane for a signal the shrunken budget reaches in about ten
  operations.* The probe above exhausts both budgets of its instance in fewer than a dozen relays.
* *Its pass or fail threshold would be a timing margin.* A soak test must decide how long is long
  enough and how slow is degraded. Both are margins that move with the machine. The suite already
  keeps timing-sensitive classes, `WebSocketProxyIT` among them, in the sequential Failsafe
  execution, because their margins do not survive CPU contention.

=== A new non-gating soak lane

Add a third lane, outside the merge path, that runs soak tests on a schedule.

Rejected. A non-gating lane reproduces the detection gap this record closes. A failure would again
arrive after the merge. Its only available reaction would again be a skip.

=== Both lanes own the defect class

Keep the gating probe and also add exhaustion goals to the benchmark lane, so each lane covers the
defect class its own way.

Rejected. One defect class gets two homes. The two definitions of "exhausted" drift apart, and a
reader cannot tell which lane is the authority. The benchmark lane keeps observing what it observes.
When it observes a resource failure, the answer is a gating test, not a second owner.

== Consequences

=== Positive

* An exhaustion defect fails the pull request that introduces it. It does not reach `main` first.
* The probe's ability to fail is demonstrated by a committed test on both budgets of its instance. A
  green probe is therefore evidence, not an untested assertion.
* The signal is deterministic. Every wait is a future completion or a bounded `get` with a timeout. No
  threshold depends on how fast the machine is.
* The benchmark lane's job stays measurement. Its budget, secrets and deploy steps stay out of the
  merge path.

=== Negative

* *Every new resource bound costs an instance or an overlay.* It needs a dedicated low-budget
  instance, or an overlay on an existing one, plus the probe, the negative control and the wiring
  guard. The integration stack grows by one gateway instance per process-wide bound. The instance
  count is already visible in the stack-startup term of the benchmark workflow's budget.
* *The low-budget instance cannot be shared with a concurrent class.* A class that holds its budget
  starves any other class on the same instance. Such a class stays in the sequential Failsafe
  execution.
* *A benchmark-observed resource failure creates work in another lane.* The benchmark lane may not
  answer it with a skip property alone. The failure obliges a gating test of the shape above.

=== Scope boundary

The decision covers some adjacent concerns and declines others:

* *The detection gap for cumulative exhaustion: adopted, narrowed.* The gating detector existed. The
  missing part was a demonstrated failure of it, which the negative control supplies.
* *The k6 scripts as unguarded consumers of the shared gateway configuration: not covered.* That is a
  consumer-drift concern of the benchmark lane, not a resource-lifecycle concern.
* *The reach of the log-message catalogue guard beyond the `api-sheriff` module: not covered.* That
  is a catalogue-completeness defect class in a different module.
* *The mTLS rejection failure in the `jfr` lane: not covered.* This decision neither detects nor
  fixes it. It is owned by the lane that runs the test, as stated above.
